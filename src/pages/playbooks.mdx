These playbooks favor a broad launch. Spend two weeks validating, then turn the app on for everyone with a Copilot seat. Use managed settings to hold back anything that isn't ready, so a single feature never delays the whole launch. Track each step in the [CoE checklist](/coe/checklist/).

## 30/60/90-day rollout

### Days 1 to 14: Validation sprint

- Name a sponsor, a CoE lead and a security partner on day 1.
- Brief admins with the [admin readiness package](/resources/#admin-readiness) and record policy decisions in the first week.
- Ship custom instructions and two or three skills in your top repositories. Stand up a private plugin marketplace with a starter kit. See [customizations](/coe/customizations/).
- Pick 20 to 50 validation users across roles, including a business team.
- Capture baseline metrics on day 1: PR cycle time, review turnaround and a pulse survey.
- Run the kickoff and get every validation user through a first session in the meeting.
- **Exit criteria:** no open blocker in admin, security or support, and a launch date set.

### Days 15 to 30: Org-wide launch

- Set managed settings for your marketplace, required plugins and MCP servers before launch day.
- Enable the app for everyone with a seat in one step.
- Send the [launch comms](/resources/#launch-comms) from the sponsor on launch day.
- Champions run a 30-minute demo in each team during launch week.
- Start daily office hours for launch week, then go to weekly. Collect crash and reliability reports there.
- **Exit criteria:** most of the org has opened the app once, and support load is steady.

### Days 31 to 90: Weekly habit

- Each team adds one automation it reads every week.
- Run a [demo day](/resources/#demo-day) for admins and decision makers in month 2.
- Publish the [which tool when](/which-tool/) guide and the starter prompt library.
- Fix the biggest drop in the activation funnel every two weeks.
- Report outcomes against the baseline with an ROI range at day 90.
- **Exit criteria:** weekly active use is climbing, and next quarter's plan is signed off.

## Team of 10

One person can run this in a few hours a week.

| Week | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Confirm seats and policies with your admin. Add a reviewed config file and `copilot-instructions.md` to the team's main repository. |
| 1 | Hold a 30-minute kickoff where everyone runs a first session on a real issue. |
| 2 | Each person picks two workflows from their [role page](/roles/), including one that isn't code, and uses them three times. |
| 2 | Set up one shared automation, such as a daily issue triage or weekly status draft. |
| 3 | Retro: keep what saved time, drop what didn't. Turn the keepers into skills. |

## Organization of 1,000

Run this as a program with a small CoE team.

| Phase | Weeks | Scope | Key activities |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Validation | 1 to 2 | 20 to 50 users across roles | Admin readiness, managed settings, marketplace, baseline, kickoff |
| Org-wide launch | 3 to 4 | Everyone with a seat, default on | Sponsor email, team demos, daily office hours, starter prompts |
| Weekly habit | 5 to 12 | Everyone | Team automations, role workshops, demo day, funnel fixes every two weeks |
| Optimize | 13 onward | Everyone | Quarterly outcome reviews, plugin catalog growth, community of practice |

Staffing guide: one CoE lead, one admin partner, one security partner and a champion for every 30 to 50 users. Line up the champions before launch day, since launch week is when they matter most.

A fast launch moves the risk from access to activation. Expect a big group who install and never start a session. Plan for it with starter prompts, team demos and the two-week reminder email, and watch the [activation funnel](/coe/measurement/#activation-funnel) daily during launch week.
